Body model and navigation are illustrative, not a medical scan. Male/female body meshes are from Blender's "Human Base Meshes" bundle (Blender Studio and community contributors, via blender.org/download/demo-files; CC0 1.0 — credited here though not legally required). Ovary/HGSOC anatomy and mutation data are drawn from TCGA (2011) and McPherson et al. (Nat Genet, 2016); breast/triple-negative data from TCGA (Nature, 2012), Yates et al. (Cancer Cell, 2017), a population-based metastatic-site study (Gao et al., Precision Medical Sciences, 2023), and a breast cancer subtype metastatic-pattern study (Kennecke et al., J Clin Oncol, 2010); lung/adenocarcinoma data from TCGA (Nature, 2014), TRACERx (Jamal-Hanjani et al., NEJM, 2017; Frankell et al., Nature, 2023), and a population-based metastatic-pattern study (Riihimäki et al., Lung Cancer, 2014); kidney/clear-cell renal cell carcinoma data from Moore et al. (PLOS Genetics, 2011), Gerlinger et al. (NEJM, 2012), TCGA (Nature, 2013), and population-based metastatic-site studies (Dabestani et al., World J Urol, 2016; Bianchi et al., Ann Oncol, 2012) — not one real patient. For all four cancers, which gene is shown at which site is a simplified teaching device: the site-pattern study establishes where spread/metastasis happens and the genomics study the cohort-level mutation frequencies, not this specific site→gene pairing.
