Body model and navigation are illustrative, not a medical scan. Male/female body meshes are from Blender's "Human Base Meshes" bundle (Blender Studio and community contributors, via blender.org/download/demo-files; CC0 1.0 — credited here though not legally required). Kidney, liver, brain, and prostate organ meshes are from NIH 3D's "Human Reference Atlas 3D Reference Object Library" (account "HRA"; CC BY 4.0 — attribution required here, unlike the body meshes above), derived from the Visible Human Dataset (Spitzer & Whitlock, Visible Human Project, 2002; Spitzer, Ackerman, Scherzinger & Whitlock, 1996) and, for the brain specifically, additionally mirroring structure from the Allen Human Brain Atlas (Ding et al., J Comp Neurol, 2016). Lungs organ mesh is "Realistic Human Lungs" by the Sketchfab artist neshallads (CC Attribution — CC BY 4.0, license confirmed directly on the model page; creator attribution required: sketchfab.com/3d-models/realistic-human-lungs-ce09f4099a68467880f46e61eb9a3531), which replaced the Human Reference Atlas lung model previously credited here because the HRA mesh has no interlobar fissures; this model sculpts the real fissure anatomy into the geometry — a deep oblique and a subtler horizontal fissure on the right lung, a single oblique fissure on the left. The larynx and thyroid gland included in the source model were removed for this lungs viewer; the lungs, trachea, and main bronchi keep the artist's original geometry and textures. Breast organ mesh is also from NIH 3D's "Human Reference Atlas 3D Reference Object Library" (CC BY 4.0) but, unlike the four organs above, is a custom hand-sculpted model rather than derived from the Visible Human Dataset scan, expert-reviewed against Krstić, Human Microscopic Anatomy (1991) and Gilroy, MacPherson & Ross, Atlas of Anatomy (2008); creator attribution (required under CC BY 4.0): Heidi Schlehlein, "3D Reference Organ for Breast (mammary gland), Female left, v1.0," 2022, doi.org/10.48539/HBM378.VWZG.633. Ovary organ mesh IS now real — the atlas's first MRI-derived organ: the left ovary isolated from "Pelvic Organs from MRI" by the Sketchfab creator audreybyrd (CC Attribution — CC BY 4.0, license confirmed on the model page, via the public API, and in the file's own embedded metadata; creator attribution required: sketchfab.com/3d-models/pelvic-organs-from-mri-2610b30793ed47a5baa18b2994bdee6b), a model "derived from a high-resolution MRI of a 25-year-old cis-female woman" produced at the Oklahoma State University Biomedical Imaging Laboratory (2022). This replaces the procedural mesh previously credited here after three earlier hunts found no usable real asset — a fourth, Sketchfab-focused hunt did. The isolation is disclosed rather than smoothed over: the source's ovary node also contains three small closed surfaces that sit inside the ovary shells (internal follicles or a corpus luteum captured by the segmentation, proven interior geometrically); they are omitted because an opaque surface render cannot show them. The mesh is uniformly rescaled so its volume is exactly 7.7 mL, anchored to the nearest well-established landmark — peak reproductive-age ovarian volume, 7.7 mL (95% CI 6.5–9.2) at age 20 (Kelsey et al., PLoS ONE, 2013, n=59,994) — stated as the model's age-20 peak rather than an exact match to the specimen's age of 25, since the reference curve declines gently after the peak. Honesty note on proportions: this real in-situ ovary is plumper and rounder (2.7 × 2.8 × 2.1 cm here) than the classic excised-almond reference (StatPearls: 3.5 × 2 × 1 cm) the old procedural mesh was built to — real anatomical variation between a live organ imaged in place, compressed by its neighbors, and an idealized reference shape; disclosed, not corrected. Its surface color remains the previously verified grayish-pink (PathologyOutlines/IMAIOS gross-anatomy descriptions) rather than the source's flat segmentation red, which is a labeling convention, not a tissue color. Testis organ mesh is likewise procedural — the same 81-entry Human Reference Atlas reference-organ library checked for every other organ in this app contains no testis, and a Sketchfab-focused hunt (2026-09-03, the same search that found the real Ovary and Stomach meshes) was also negative: the only anatomically-faithful candidate found is licensed CC BY-NC-SA, which this cleanly-CC-BY atlas does not adopt — sized to StatPearls' own measurement ("Anatomy, Abdomen and Pelvis: Testes": 3–5cm long, 2–3cm wide; depth assumed equal to width, stated as an assumption rather than a further citation). Bladder organ mesh IS real: NIH 3D's "Human Reference Atlas 3D Reference Object Library" (CC BY 4.0), the male urinary-bladder reference organ, derived like the kidney/liver/brain/prostate meshes from the Visible Human Dataset (Spitzer & Whitlock, 2002; Spitzer, Ackerman, Scherzinger & Whitlock, 1996) — checked for an individually-documented creator/DOI the way the Colon and Pancreas meshes have; none is published for this asset, so it is attributed at the level the source actually documents, the same as the four organs above it. Its six named anatomical sub-meshes (bladder neck, dome, trigone, and both ureteral orifices, each carrying its own UBERON/FMA ontology id) are used directly: every hotspot in this organ's 3D view is anchored to that sub-mesh's real vertex centroid, not a hand-placed estimate. Colon (large intestine) organ mesh is "Small and large intestine" by the Sketchfab artist antonia.sundberg (CC Attribution — CC BY 4.0, license confirmed both on the model page and in the file's own embedded metadata; creator attribution required: sketchfab.com/3d-models/small-and-large-intestine-8a1ca8e3ca224cdeb9264674416bde38; made for a scientific-illustration course at Mälardalen University), which replaced the Human Reference Atlas large-intestine model previously credited here because the HRA mesh — while colonoscopy-derived and real — lacks the colon's two most identifying external features: its haustra are only faintly suggested and its taeniae coli are absent entirely (confirmed source-exhausted: HRA's newer release is vertex-identical). This model carries real geometric haustra, a modeled taenia coli band, the cecum, appendix, and rectum; the small-intestine mesh included in the source was removed for this viewer, and the large intestine keeps the artist's original geometry and textures. Pancreas organ mesh is from NIH 3D's "Human Reference Atlas 3D Reference Object Library" (CC BY 4.0), shipped as the original HRA-authored file, byte-identical to upstream; creator attribution (required under CC BY 4.0): Kristen Browne; Heidi Schlehlein, "3D Reference Organ for Pancreas, Female v1.2," 2023, doi.org/10.48539/HBM938.BRGC.644, "Accessed on December 15, 2023." Stomach organ mesh IS now real — "Realistic Stomach" by the Sketchfab artist Brain Diagno (CC Attribution — CC BY 4.0, license confirmed on the model page, via the public API, and in the file's own embedded metadata; creator attribution required: sketchfab.com/3d-models/realistic-stomach-07859d72489d4f818e508b3738ab7449), replacing the procedural mesh previously credited here after earlier hunts (the HRA library contains no stomach at all) were finally beaten by a Sketchfab-focused search. It is an artist-authored anatomical model, not a scan — the weakest provenance class among this atlas's real meshes, adopted because its landmarks were verified as real geometry rather than on author authority: flat-shaded texture-free renders confirm the convex greater curvature, concave lesser curvature with incisura, fundus, antral taper, and open esophageal and duodenal stubs (both kept, as the Lungs' trachea and Colon's rectum stub were; the duodenal loop is generously long, disclosed rather than trimmed). Scale is anchored to a verified real measurement in a stated way: the greatest diameter is set to 10.4 cm (Cunningham's Text-book of Anatomy, 1905: "not more than 4 to 4.5 inches (10 to 11.2 cm.)"), which lands the overall extent at 25.4 cm (Cunningham's 25–27.5 cm length) and the enclosed volume at a coherent moderately-full 216 mL — a volume anchor was deliberately rejected because gastric volume ranges from ~25 mL empty to liters full, so no single volume describes the organ. Its painted surface texture (including the visible vessel tracery) is the artist's own work, not independently color-verified — the same disclosure as the Lungs, Colon, and Thyroid textures — and the previous procedural mesh's serosal-color inference note is thereby retired with it, since no source states the gastric serosa's color in words. Thyroid organ mesh is "TIROIDES ANDREA DACS UJAT" by the Sketchfab artist andycopo55 (CC Attribution — CC BY 4.0, license confirmed both on the model page and in the file's own embedded metadata; creator attribution required: sketchfab.com/3d-models/tiroides-andrea-dacs-ujat-1e1f5ab5d1f348dba7ef3215b22cc37f; made at Universidad Juárez Autónoma de Tabasco), the third artist-authored mesh here after Lungs and Colon — the HRA reference-organ library contains no thyroid at all (checked directly, not assumed). The source file is a full neck assembly; the trachea, laryngeal cartilage, and vessels were removed for this viewer, and the gland keeps the artist's original geometry and texture. Its size is anchored to a verified real measurement in a stated way: uniformly rescaled so its ellipsoid-formula lobe volume equals the adult median total thyroid volume (8.26 mL — Lin et al., Biol Trace Elem Res, 2023), which lands per-lobe width essentially at reference (1.34 vs 1.31 cm). Honesty note on proportions: at that verified volume the model's lobes run shorter top-to-bottom (2.77 cm) and correspondingly deeper front-to-back than textbook proportions — a stylization disclosed rather than silently "corrected," because a correction would have needed a target lobe length no checked source could verify (StatPearls' own size sentence, ~25 g yet 6.6 mL, is internally inconsistent, and the common "5 × 3 × 2 cm per lobe" paraphrase implies a volume above the reference study's upper limit, i.e. a goitre). The isthmus landmark was verified as real geometry, not texture, before integration. Skin is shown as a procedural schematic cross-section block, not a scan — a real whole-body skin model exists in the HRA library (CC BY 4.0) but was measured and deliberately not used: it is a single outer-surface shell with none of the layered structure melanoma arises in, and skin covers the whole body rather than sitting in one place. The block is illustrative in both overall size and layer proportions — nothing about it is to scale: layer thicknesses are visually exaggerated for legibility (measured epidermis 0.03–0.6 mm, Lintzeri et al., 2022; dermis 1.5–4 mm, whole skin ~2 mm average); its layer colors follow published descriptions — white dermis (Liu et al., 2023; the conventional diagram pink could not be verified in any source), light-yellow subcutaneous fat (surgical-literature descriptions, the weakest sourcing tier here) — and its surface tone is one point on a real continuum: skin tones differ by melanin amount, not melanocyte count (StatPearls). Its body-screen marker sits at a different verified site per sex (trunk in men, lower limbs in women — CONCORD-3). For all sixteen cancers, which gene is shown at which site is a simplified teaching device: the site-pattern study establishes where spread/metastasis happens (or, for glioblastoma, where within one tumor mass regional heterogeneity happens instead, and for prostate acinar adenocarcinoma, where independently-arising tumor foci within one gland happen instead) and the genomics study the cohort-level mutation frequencies, not this specific site→gene pairing.
